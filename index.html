AgenticPay: A Multimodal Benchmark for LLM-Powered Negotiation in Multi-Agent Commerce

Xianyang Liu1, Shangding Gu1, Dawn Song1
1University of California Berkeley
Paper Code Tutorial Issue

Figure 1: AgenticPay framework overview.

Abstract

AgenticPay is a framework and benchmark for evaluating LLM/VLM agents in realistic buyer-seller commerce. It extends negotiation beyond text-only, bilateral price haggling into multimodal, multi-dimensional contract negotiation across four real-world scenarios: E-commerce, Food Delivery, Ride-hailing, and Apartment Rental. The benchmark covers eight market topologies, from one-to-one bargaining to many-to-many markets, while keeping a Gymnasium-like API for reproducible evaluation and extensible environment registration.

Benchmark Features

  • LLM/VLM-based agents: Buyer and seller agents powered by text and vision-language models, including OpenAI-compatible APIs, vLLM, SGLang, and Qwen3-VL.
  • Multimodal product grounding: Tasks can include product images, visual route context, listings, menus, and rich text attributes.
  • Multi-dimensional contracts: Agents negotiate complete JSON contracts with price, continuous terms, and discrete terms instead of a single scalar price.
  • Multi-turn conversations: The benchmark supports extended natural-language negotiation dialogues with structured contract proposals.
  • Utility-based metrics: GlobalScore, BuyerScore, and SellerScore evaluate feasibility, welfare, surplus split, and efficiency.
  • 160 benchmark tasks: Four scenarios times eight market topologies times five tasks, with additional legacy and demo scripts.

Scenarios and Tasks

AgenticPay studies realistic commerce settings across E-commerce, Food Delivery, Ride-hailing, and Apartment Rental. These scenarios instantiate single-buyer/single-seller negotiation, multi-product settings, multi-seller competition, multi-buyer demand, and more complex many-to-many interactions.

Figure 2: Scenario and task categories.

Evaluation

AgenticPay evaluates negotiation outcomes through contract feasibility, buyer utility, seller utility, social welfare, agreement quality, and negotiation efficiency. The benchmark is designed to make commerce agents measurable under multimodal information, private preferences, market competition, and structured contract constraints.
